## What it does

The app adds an age gate to your storefront. Visitors confirm they are old enough, or enter their
year of birth, before they see the store or a restricted product. Nothing about them is sent to
AnonAge or anyone else: the answer is kept in a single cookie in their own browser, holding the age
they passed (for example `18`), never a date of birth.

**Age Gate** is US$2 per month, billed through Shopify, with a 14-day free trial. Verified age checks
through the AnonAge app are coming later as a separate plan.

<Warning>
The age gate is a **self-declaration overlay**. It is a courtesy notice, not a lock: anyone can
answer untruthfully, and a visitor can remove an overlay with browser developer tools or by turning
JavaScript off. If the law requires you to verify ages, a self-declared answer is not enough on its
own.
</Warning>

## Set up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install and approve the plan">
    The 14-day trial starts when you approve it. Uninstalling before it ends costs nothing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn the gate on in your theme">
    The app's home page has a button that opens the theme editor at **App embeds** with the gate
    selected. Switch it on and save.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose where it applies">
    The whole store, or selected products and collections. Tagging a product `age-restricted` is the
    quickest way to gate it; you can also pick products and collections directly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check it as a visitor">
    Open your store in a private window. Once you have answered the gate in a browser it stays out of
    your way there for as long as you chose to remember the answer, so a normal window will not show
    it again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Restricted products in listings

When the gate covers selected products, visitors who have not passed it also see those products
treated differently wherever they are listed. You choose the treatment separately for the home page
and for everywhere else (collections, search, recommendations and menus):

| Treatment | What visitors see |
|---|---|
| Hide them | The product is left out of the listing. Default on the home page. |
| Blur and label them | The card is blurred and labelled "Age restricted: 18+". Default elsewhere. |
| Grey them out | The card is shown in grey, faded. |
| Show them normally | No change. |

Blurred and greyed products still open when clicked, behind the gate.

### Use an obscured featured image

Blurring a card on the page does not hide the photo itself: the real image is still the one your
store sends, and anyone can open it in a new tab. If the photo must not be seen in listings at all,
give the product a different **featured image**.

The [restricted image maker](https://anonage.io/tools/restricted-image) turns a product photo into a
blurred or pixelated, labelled version. It works in your browser and does not upload your photo.
The saved image is built from a version a few dozen pixels wide, so the detail is not hidden in it:
it is not there.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make the image">
    Open the [restricted image maker](https://anonage.io/tools/restricted-image), choose your photo,
    adjust it and download it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it to the product">
    In Shopify admin, open the product and add the image under **Media**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it the first image">
    Drag it to the first position. The first image is the featured image used on the home page,
    collections and search. Keep your real photos after it; they show on the product page, behind
    the gate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
If you sell through Google, Meta or other sales channels, they are sent the featured image too, and
some reject placeholder or obscured product images. Check their image rules before switching.
</Tip>

## Limits worth knowing

- Picked collections contribute their first 50 products to the listing treatment, and the tag is
  checked across the first 250 products in your store. Products past those limits are listed
  normally, though their own pages are still gated.
- Product cards are recognised by the list item a theme puts around each one, as Dawn and most
  themes do. A theme that lists products some other way is left alone.
